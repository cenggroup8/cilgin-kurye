\msection{Purpose}

M5 is the web page on each player's phone. The player scans a QR code or types the game code, chooses
a team, and drives a motorcycle in a low poly 3D city with buttons on the screen. There is no map on
the phone. Only a compass arrow shows the direction of the delivery point or of the store. So players
must look at the LED board on the table to find their way.

\ckinfo{cli}{Player phone, only the web browser, nothing to install. The page comes from M2}{TypeScript web page with Three.js for 3D, low poly models from one Blender file shared with the board buildings, MessagePack messages, the M3 physics code for prediction}{R1 R2 R3 R5 R7 R8 R10 R11}

The phone talks only to M2. It gets the page once over HTTP and then keeps one WebSocket open. The
figure shows the data in both directions.

\msection{How it works}

\msub{The game screen}
The phone is held sideways. The 3D view fills the screen, and the camera
follows the motorcycle from behind. At the top the player sees the time left and the score of each
team. In the top corner there is the compass. The arrow points to the delivery point, or to the
store when the player has no package. It never shows the distance or the roads. The buttons are
large so that the thumbs can find them without looking: left and right on one side, gas and brake
on the other side. There is no delivery button: when the player stops at the delivery point, the
package is delivered automatically. The ``Find me'' button asks the server to flash the
player's LED on the board in white for 1 second. After a press, the button is grey and shows the
waiting time that M1 puts into the game state.

\msub{Smooth play on a real network}
The server sends a state 30 times per second, but Wi-Fi adds
delay. If the phone waited for the server, the motorcycle would react late to a button. So the phone
moves its own motorcycle at once with the M3 code, and corrects it when the real state arrives. For
the other players and the NPC cars, the phone keeps the last few states and draws them a little in
the past. Then it can always move them smoothly between two real states, and they do not jump.

\msub{The 3D city}
The city is built from the same city plan as the LED board: 6 roads in each
direction, 60 road segments and 25 blocks. Tall buildings stand in the centre, houses and trees are
outside, and the four team stores in the corners have the team colours. The models are low poly and
come from one Blender file. The board uses the same models for 3D printing, so a player can find the same building on the board and on the phone. Objects that
repeat, like trees and houses, are drawn as instances, so a mid range phone can draw a smooth picture. During an earthquake the camera shakes, and the
shaking is stronger near the centre of the earthquake.

\msub{Obstacles and falls in the 3D city}
Obstacles from the game state are drawn on their road: cones and a barrier for road work, crashed
cars for an accident, slow cars for heavy traffic and a wet patch for a water puddle. So a change by
the admin is seen in the 3D city in the same tick as on the board. A fallen motorcycle lies on the
road, and the page shows a penalty countdown until M1 lets it stand up again.

\msection{Testing}

M5 is tested alone against a fake server that sends recorded game states and answers the join and
the controls. We check joining on Android and iPhone, every button and every screen, the compass
target, the ``Find me'' waiting time, reconnecting after a page reload with the same player key, and
the prediction with added delay and loss. We also check that a mid range phone draws the full city
smoothly.
